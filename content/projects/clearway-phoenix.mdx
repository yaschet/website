Hundreds of students were waiting for university recommendations at Clearway, a study-abroad agency. Advisors spent hours piecing together options from spreadsheets where fees, admission rules and scholarship conditions were buried in inconsistent notes. Students finished a call and waited days for recommendations.

I built Phoenix so advisors could search and rank thousands of programs in real time, explain the recommendations and save a student's shortlist during the call. They can review grades, admission requirements, fees and scholarships together, then adjust the search as they learn more about the student.

The stakes were the student's education and finances. A missed admission requirement, misunderstood scholarship or overlooked program could lead them toward an unsuitable choice. I made the ranking transparent so an advisor could inspect the reasons, check the conditions and discuss the trade-offs with the student before recommending a program. The agency's reputation depended on that quality of advice.

The first matching tool relied on grade calculations and exact keywords. A search for economics could miss a business program because the wording was different. The database also lacked the detail needed to compare admission and scholarship conditions. Improving the search alone would not fix the information underneath it.

I rebuilt Phoenix as three connected parts. First, I designed the database and the screens staff would use to manage universities, programs, accommodation and scholarships. Then I built a resumable AI importer to turn the existing spreadsheets into connected records. Finally, I built the program search, scoring and advising workspace on top of that information.

I owned product design and engineering from requirements through production deployment inside the agency's existing Laravel platform: the database, management screens, AI imports, matching logic and complete advising interface.

## Rebuild The University And Admissions Database

The information was spread across university, program, accommodation and scholarship sheets. Names contained typos and abbreviations. Prices appeared as single amounts, ranges or several degree-specific fees in one cell. Admission and funding conditions were written as prose. Advisors had to interpret the information before they could even compare programs.

I designed the database to connect each program with its university, admission requirements, accommodation and available funding. The software could then distinguish what a scholarship pays for, who qualifies, how long it lasts and what renewal requires. Programs had defined grade and language requirements. Subjects belonged to broader fields, giving the search a way to connect related courses.

I also designed and built a management system for universities, programs, accommodation and scholarships. Staff could create, review, edit and remove records through the application. It included forms, searchable and filterable tables, expandable details, and settings for subjects, intakes, language tests and scholarships. Reusable requirements kept repeated information consistent, while AI autofill could prefill university and program details from a few inputs.

Staff could maintain the information themselves. A changed admission requirement or a new scholarship could be updated through the application and used in future recommendations.

## Import The Spreadsheets Without Starting Over After A Failure

The hardest part was interpreting what each cell meant. A row that looked simple in a spreadsheet could describe several records with different rules:

- A fee cell could list different charges for different degree levels, with inconsistent labels and formatting.
- Scholarship notes could describe several awards, each with its own coverage, duration and eligibility. Some entries ran together without spaces or line breaks.
- Accommodation notes mixed room types, facilities and extra charges.
- Language-test requirements could name a test without giving the minimum score.

Copying those cells into a database would preserve the confusion. I needed to separate the facts, put each one in the right field and retain the links between universities, programs, accommodation and funding.

I built an AI importer to process the spreadsheets row by row, interpret those entries, validate the extracted information and populate the new database. It created universities before their accommodation, programs and scholarships, preserving the links between them as spreadsheet references became connected records.

Running AI across thousands of rows made failures expensive. I saved completed transformations so unchanged rows could reuse their earlier results, and added checkpoints so an interrupted import could continue from saved progress. Temporary errors received a limited number of retries. Failed rows stayed available to inspect and correct.

That meant a failure near the end did not require starting the entire import again or paying for the same successful AI calls twice.

## Find Suitable Programs And Show Why They Match

With the information imported and maintainable, I could build the search and scoring system on top of it. A student interested in economics should be able to discover relevant business programs even when the course names use different words. I combined MeiliSearch with OpenAI embeddings so the search could match meaning as well as wording. I then built the rules that compare the results with the student's grades and preferences.

Finding a related subject was only the first step. A program could fit the student's interests while requiring grades they did not have, offering unsuitable funding or approaching its application deadline. I built a ranking model with 13 weighted criteria, including academic fit, subject relevance, scholarship potential, location and deadlines.

I made each match score explainable in the interface. Advisors can open the breakdown to see what raised or reduced the score, including penalties for unmet grade requirements. Critical conditions stay visible alongside the percentage, so the advisor has the information needed to judge the recommendation.

Each program is assessed against the same criteria. The breakdown makes the reasoning open to review: the advisor can see how the student's profile affects the ranking and which conditions still need attention. That gives them a consistent basis for comparing options, explaining the trade-offs and checking the recommendation before adding it to the student's shortlist.

## Design The Workspace Around The Advising Call

An advisor needs to compare options while speaking with a student. I designed three connected areas: a searchable student list, the selected student's profile and program results, and filters with the student's assigned programs. Grades, study interests and recommendations can be reviewed together.

During a call, the advisor can adjust the student's information, narrow the results and inspect a program without leaving the workspace. Selecting a program adds it to the student's record. The advisor can then change its priority and update its application status as the student moves forward.

Recommendations had to arrive fast enough to keep the conversation moving. I optimized the search and results screen to return the first recommendations in under a second, fetching and rendering a small batch rather than every result. More programs load as the advisor scrolls.

I built the interface with Livewire and Alpine.js inside the existing Laravel platform. Switching students, changing filters and assigning programs update the relevant part of the screen without reloading the page. An advisor can keep refining the options with the student in front of them.

## Keep Recommendations Connected To Applications

I deployed the work in stages: the database and management screens, the spreadsheet importer, then the matching and advising interface. Each stage supported the next, from making the university information usable to putting recommendations in an advisor's hands.

The change is from hours of manual cross-checking and a follow-up days later to searching, comparing and shortlisting during the call. Advisors can respond to the student's questions as they arise, show why an option fits and surface conditions that need attention. Each shortlist stays attached to the student, ready for the team to manage the applications.

The team can also maintain the university information and import new spreadsheets without rebuilding the process. Fast recommendations depend on information staff can keep accurate and up to date.

The later student portal feeds extracted grades into the same student records. Document collection, program recommendations and application tracking can therefore use the same information inside the agency's platform.
